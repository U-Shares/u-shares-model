# Evidence — F7: The poverty-first ladder

**Claim (register):** The reach is a poverty-first ladder served by one affordable vehicle, scaling with
growth and the dedicated share (structure only; magnitudes held).
**Status: `contested`** — the deepest-tier contest is presented in full below.
**Warrant (proposed):** filling finite capacity depth-first up the income tiers delivers the poorest
first at any budget, and equal-budget modeling shows principal-holding compresses inequality more than
universal transfers — *contingent on placement not destroying means-tested benefits at the bottom.*

## Derivation
1. **Capacity → reach.** Steady-state placeable stock S_max = s·R/c (dedicated share × incremental
   receipts ÷ ongoing deductibility rate), filled depth-first: poverty (~36M) → near-poor ≤200% FPL
   (~61M) → lower half → universal, capped per person. Structure (the *order* and the *scaling*) is the
   claim; per-person magnitudes are held (M0). Model: `track_d_distribution.py`.
2. **Why poverty-first (not merely chosen — measured).** At equal budget, directional ABM comparisons
   show bottom-targeted placement compresses the top/bottom gap more than universal spreading, because
   compression comes from *held principal* at the bottom, not the size of any check. Models:
   `universal_distribution.py`, `q1_lever_vs_fund.py` (ECON-005/Q1 line).
3. **Tier populations** are approximate (Census SPM alignment pending — flagged in the OQ register).

## Sensitivity
Reach scales ~linearly in s and R and inversely in c (holder-mix dependent); the employment regime gates
R entirely (F1) — displacement zeroes the ladder. Depth-first vs spread-thin is a policy dial, not a
model result; the compression finding holds under either but is strongest depth-first.

## The contest (presented fairly — why the status is `contested`)
**The deepest-tier inversion (WS-4, household persona; the KS1 finding).** For households on SSI (and
SSI-linked Medicaid), the $2,000 resource limit and dollar-for-dollar offsets mean an unprotected asset
placement can *destroy more benefit value than it creates* — the ladder inverts exactly where it claims
to help first. **Verified against current law (2026-07-18):** enacted §530A (Trump-Account) practice
shows the fix *for minors* — SSA excludes the account from SSI tests during the growth period — but that
exclusion is guidance-level (revocable), ends at adulthood (post-17 treatment "forthcoming"), with a
disability-gated ABLE rollover (disregard to $100k) as the escape. **No adult disregard exists in current
law.** The claim therefore carries a stated design requirement: a **statutory, lifetime asset-disregard**
(generalizing ABLE's model and §530A's childhood precedent). Without it, poverty-first is not true at the
deepest tier; with it, the precedent chain (ABLE → §530A → U-Shares) is enacted-law-adjacent, not novel.
An earlier internal framing that booked deep-tier benefit losses as fiscal savings has been withdrawn.

## Disconfirming test (register)
Validated capacity/reach modeling (pinned to Census SPM tiers) contradicting the ladder structure — or a
demonstration that, at the deepest tier, means-tested benefit cliffs make placement wealth-destroying
absent an asset-disregard provision.
