# Evidence — GV1: Governance can make the reserve un-raidable (proposed, not proven)

**Claim (register):** A governance design can keep the combined reserve un-raidable across
administrations (structure proposed; not yet specified to counsel/institutional grade).
**Status: `open`.**
**Warrant (proposed):** the failure base-rate shows capture and lawful redefinition — not markets — kill
public funds; a narrow, on-budget, statutorily-insulated insurer minimizes the attack surface by design,
though no design is capture-proof and none is yet specified to counsel grade.

## The evidence base (the durability study)
1. **The failure pattern is governance, not markets.** 1MDB (>$4.5B embezzled → insolvent by 2016);
   Venezuela FONDEN (~$30B unaccounted; off-budget, answerable only to the executive); Libya LIA — the
   isolating case: its sanctions-*frozen* assets grew ~12% while its politically-managed subsidiaries
   *lost* value. You cannot out-invest capture. The common enabling flaw: opacity + off-budget structure +
   concentrated political control without independent oversight. The durable funds (Norway, Singapore,
   Alaska's Permanent *Fund*) share the mirror-image traits.
2. **The American failure mode is lawful redefinition, not fraud.** Alaska's citizen *dividend* — a
   statutory formula — was capped by gubernatorial veto under deficit stress in 2016 and the override was
   upheld (*Wielechowski v. State*, 2017). Nothing illegal happened. A statutory promise is not
   self-enforcing against a legislature that needs the money; guidance-level protections (cf. the §530A
   SSI exclusion, F7) are weaker still. This is the harder problem, and the register's disconfirming test
   now benchmarks both base rates.
3. **Why capture can't be reserved against (the design implication).** Modeled as a jump process, even a
   low capture hazard with high loss dominates the market-tail expected loss by roughly an order of
   magnitude (illustrative arithmetic, durability findings §3). Capital does not fix it; *design* must:
   the U-Share vehicle is therefore specified as a **narrow insurer** — a defined claim (pay the floor
   shortfall, recover from the issuer), no active political investment mission, no share-voting for
   policy ends, on-budget, independently audited, with mechanical claim rules and statutory (ideally
   constitutional/contractual) anti-raid protection. Smaller, narrower, more rule-bound → less to raid.
4. **Live confirmation.** The U.S. EO-14196 fund effort stalled on "legal, financial, and political
   realities" — institutional hurdles, not the investment case (status as of mid-2026; currency pass
   re-verifies).

## Honest limits (why `open`)
The design principles are extracted from case evidence, not proven: no statutory draft exists, no counsel
review, no institutional-grade specification; the base rate is a pattern across prominent cases, not a
clean denominator; the jump arithmetic is illustrative. And *no* design is capture-proof — the claim is
that capture can be made hard and visible, not impossible.

## Disconfirming test (register)
A political-economy analysis showing no statutory/constitutional design survives a deficit-running
sovereign, benchmarked against both the raided-fund base rate (1MDB, FONDEN, LIA) and the domestic
lawful-redefinition base rate (Alaska PFD / *Wielechowski v. State*, 2017).
