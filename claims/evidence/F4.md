# Evidence — F4: Issuance is not the binding constraint

**Claim (register):** Issuance is not the binding constraint — the credit attaches to *placement*, so
firms issue on ordinary financing merits.
**Status: `supported`** — with the residual (adoption) stated plainly. WS-4 flagged this claim's
evidence→claim bridge as the register's weakest; this file is that bridge, made explicit.
**Warrant (proposed):** issuance and targeting are separable decisions — the placement credit carries the
public-purpose burden, so the firm's decision reduces to ordinary financing economics, which most firms
clear across the cycle (F5) and which debt cannot substitute for (permanence).

## Derivation (the bridge, step by step)
1. **Decouple.** The credit attaches to *placement* (getting shares into qualifying accounts), not to
   issuance. The firm's decision is therefore only: is issuing U-Shares good financing? The demanding,
   recession-fragile discount-to-poverty path (~7.58% break-even) is never the firm's to clear — the
   credit funds targeting.
2. **The financing economics.** The 5% coupon is deductible (REIT-precedent), so the after-tax cost is
   5% × (1−21%) = **3.95%**. A firm deploying the capital above that after-tax return gains by issuing.
   Most of the corporate universe clears this bar across the full cycle (F5's measurement).
3. **Why "cheaper debt" does not defeat it (the WS-4 challenge, rebutted).** The adverse-selection
   objection benchmarked U-Shares against refinanceable debt. But (a) **permanence:** debt must be
   *repaid* — it is temporary capital with refinancing risk and a senior liquidation claim; U-Share
   capital is *permanent equity* with different rights in cash flow and liquidation. They are not
   substitutes, so a debt-cost comparison does not bind the issuance decision. (b) **Rates:** even on
   debt's own terms, both coupons are deductible, so the comparison is 5% vs the nominal debt rate —
   crossover ≈5% nominal; at elevated rates U-Shares are parity-or-cheaper anyway. (c) Against *common
   equity* (~8–10% cost), U-Shares are decisively cheaper. The mandatory floor is insured (F6) with
   issuer recourse, so the instrument does not carry a hidden distress discount for holders.
4. **Scale sanity.** Program placement demand is a fraction of a percent of the equity base per year;
   with the large majority of firms clearing the bar, willing-issuer supply exceeds program demand by
   orders of magnitude. Quantity is not the constraint; the incentive points the right way.

## Sensitivity
The 3.95% bar moves with the corporate rate (21%); the rate-crossover point moves with nominal yields —
the *permanence* argument is rate-independent, the *rate-parity* argument is cyclical. Clearance
measurement caveats live in F5.

## The honest residual (why the disconfirming test is adoption)
Clearing the bar is **necessary, not sufficient**: a firm that *can* profitably issue may still not —
awareness, transaction cost, credit-monetization friction, market appetite, and control/dilution concerns
are a behavioral layer no model here estimates (OQ-MD-002's stated answer-shape). That is the claim's
genuine open edge, and the register's disconfirming test keys to it.

## Disconfirming test (register)
Firm-level evidence that, despite favorable issuance economics, firms do not adopt at the scale the
mechanism needs (awareness, transaction cost, market appetite, control/dilution concerns).
