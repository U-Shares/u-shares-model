# Evidence — F6: One combined reserve (floor backstop + recession deductibility + systemic reinsurance)

**Claim (register):** The dividend floor, the recession deductibility cost, and systemic reinsurance can
be met by *one* combined reserve, seedable from public assets (structure only; size held).
**Status: `contested`** — the contest is presented in full below, not adjudicated here.
**Warrant (proposed):** two independent models — a correlated-default backstop tail and a deficit-guardrail
recession stress — size the floor, deductibility, and systemic needs to the *same order of magnitude*, so
a single pool can plausibly serve all three jobs.

## Derivation (two independent routes to the same order)
1. **Backstop tail (floor guarantee).** One-factor Gaussian copula (Vasicek/Basel ASRF; the standard
   correlated-default framework). Issuers "default" on the floor with annual probability PD, joint-defaults
   driven by asset correlation ρ; annual loss = default-fraction × 5% coupon × LGD. Reserve target =
   Expected Shortfall at a 1-in-200 tolerance. Swept PD 1–5%, ρ 0.10–0.30: ES99.5 spans ~0.35%–2.36% of
   insured notional (central ~1.0%). Model: `tail_reserve_model.py` (fixed seed; 2M scenarios).
2. **Recession deductibility (the guardrail's standing cost).** The 5% dividend's corporate deductibility
   (~1.05%/yr of placed stock) runs in recessions when the growth-receipts budget is zero. Reserve to ride
   a 2-year receipts dropout at steady state computes to the *same order* as route 1. Model:
   `deficit_guardrail.py`.
3. **Convergence.** The two routes were built from different premises (insurance tail vs fiscal-flow
   stress) and were not fitted to each other; their agreement in order of magnitude is the claim's core
   evidence. A third job — reinsuring the rare correlated tail — sizes inside the same pool's stress runs.
4. **Seedability.** Federal income-generating assets (energy/mineral/spectrum rents, capitalized) fall in
   the same order — far too small to *pay* a floor (a fund paying the floor outright requires roughly two
   orders of magnitude more), right-sized to *reserve* one.

## Sensitivity
Reserve scales ~linearly with insured notional (which ramps from ~zero under voluntary issuance) and
roughly with PD×LGD; ρ drives the tail shape. Recession-persistence (AR(1) φ) worsens ruin modestly;
regime-switching untested (flagged). Recovery on failed-issuer claims (PBGC-style subrogation — the
dividend is mandatory; the entity pays and seeks reimbursement) roughly halves net reserve need at 50%
recovery.

## The contest (presented fairly — why the status is `contested`)
- **The draw-state borrowing objection (WS-4, fiscal persona).** A Treasuries-invested reserve liquidated
  in a claim year is, on a consolidated federal balance sheet, *borrowing in the draw state* — so "one
  affordable reserve" cannot be read as costless safety capital. The structural convergence stands; the
  *affordability* framing must carry the consolidated-balance-sheet cost, which no model here prices.
- **No unified model.** The convergence is between two separate models; a single unified ALM/ruin model
  (regime-switching, negative inflation/return covariance) with credentialed (G3) sign-off does not yet
  exist — and the register's disconfirming test keys to exactly that.
- **Magnitude held (M0).** The dollar figure is deliberately not asserted; "same order" is the claim's
  entire quantitative content.

## Disconfirming test (register)
A unified ALM/ruin model in which the three needs don't size together, or the pool is unaffordable — or a
consolidated-balance-sheet accounting in which the reserve's draw-state borrowing cost makes the combined
vehicle unaffordable.
