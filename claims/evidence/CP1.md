# Evidence — CP1: Comparative positioning (with the dimensions where U-Shares is worse)

**Claim (register):** U-Shares occupies a defensible position vs the ownership-broadening family (baby
bonds, ESOPs, UBI, citizen-capitalism variants) — including stated dimensions where it is *worse*.
**Status: `open`** — this file *is* the comparison draft (Item 3); it is a structured argument, not a
settled ranking.
**Warrant (proposed):** enacted §530A accounts prove the account architecture (individual account, public
seed, equity, lock); U-Shares differs by corporate issuance, the insured floor, and adult reach — the
position is defensible only with its worse-dimensions stated, which this comparison does.

## The comparison set (anchored to enacted law first)
**Trump Accounts (§530A, enacted 2025; open July 2026)** — the *enacted anchor*: individual protected
account, $1,000 public seed (2025–28 cohort), broad-index equity, statutory lock to 18, IRA treatment
with conversion features; SSA disregards the account for SSI during childhood (guidance-level). Then:
**baby bonds** (Booker-style; state pilots) · **ESOPs** (decades of data; §1042) · **UBI / citizen
dividends** (Alaska PFD; the Sanders AI-fund bill) · **Stout-style citizen capitalism** (issuance-based,
heritable individual ownership) · **MOSAIC/UHI-type proposals** (ownership vs transfer hybrids) · **intelligence-endowment programs** (*The Last Economy*'s universal claim on frontier AI capability — Intelligent Internet, 2025; see the dedicated section below).

## The structured comparison (direction, not magnitude)

| Dimension | U-Shares vs the family |
|---|---|
| **Dispersion power** | Strongest *in design*: held principal + retention wrapper + poverty-first placement is the direct dispersion mechanism (DS1). Baby bonds share the logic at smaller scale; UBI transfers income, not holdings; ESOPs disperse only within employed firms. |
| **Funding engine** | Unique: corporate issuance on financing merits (F4/F5) rather than appropriations. Baby bonds/UBI compete for tax dollars annually; §530A seeds are appropriated and cohort-limited. |
| **Downside protection** | Unique: the insured 5% floor (F6). No comparator protects the stake's income. |
| **Adult reach** | U-Shares targets adults-in-poverty first (F7); §530A and baby bonds are children-only; ESOPs reach only employees; UBI reaches all but builds no stake. |
| **Delivery infrastructure** | Now largely *shared*: §530A proves the rails; a placement layer can ride them (the "carry it forward" thesis). |
| **Evidence base** | **Worst of the family** — see below. |
| **Simplicity / political lift** | **Near-worst** — see below. |

## Where U-Shares is honestly WORSE (the claim's load-bearing candor)
1. **Zero real-world evidence.** UBI has randomized pilots; ESOPs have decades of outcome data; the PFD
   has 40+ years of distribution history; §530A is enacted law. U-Shares has directional models and no
   implementation anywhere. On demonstrated-effect, it ranks last.
2. **Complexity.** A new instrument + a transferable credit + an insurance entity + a statutory disregard
   is the most moving parts of any comparator. A UBI check is radically simpler; a baby bond nearly so.
   Complexity is attack surface — legal (L1), administrative, and political.
3. **Dependence on voluntary corporate adoption.** Baby bonds and UBI need no firm to act; U-Shares'
   scale rests on issuance take-up, which is economically favorable but behaviorally unproven (F4's open
   edge).
4. **Benefits-cliff exposure.** As an *asset-building* program it inherits the means-test cliff at the
   deepest tier absent the statutory disregard (F7/OQ-HH-004); income-style transfers hit income tests
   but do not strand a locked asset.
5. **Legal classification risk.** The floor's equity-not-debt status is unresolved (L1); UBI and baby
   bonds carry no analogous instrument-level legal risk.
6. **Political lift.** Requires major tax legislation; comparators can advance incrementally (§530A
   expansions, state baby-bond programs).

**The honest synthesis:** U-Shares is best understood not as a rival to the family but as its
*completion layer* — riding §530A's rails, adding what no comparator has (the issuance engine, the
insured floor, adult poverty-first reach), and paying for that with the family's highest complexity and
thinnest evidence base. "Defensible position" means exactly this trade, stated plainly.

## Disconfirming test (register)
A structured comparison showing an existing instrument dominates U-Shares on every stated dimension.
*(The table above shows the converse claim's shape: no comparator currently dominates on all dimensions —
but several beat U-Shares on evidence, simplicity, and lift, which is why the claim is `open`, not
`supported`.)*

## The intelligence-endowment member *(added 2026-07-22 — *The Last Economy* study)*
*The Last Economy* (Intelligent Internet, 2025) contributes the family's only **intelligence-endowment**
member: a universal claim on frontier AI capability — an endowment of the productive *instrument* rather
than of capital returns. On this comparison's dimensions it is the **complement, not a competitor**: it
supplies what no ownership program here addresses (guaranteed access to the means of production), and it
omits what U-Shares supplies (a compounding claim on capital returns — the property leg its own
r-vs-g-as-topology diagnosis demands — verified by full-text sweep). Stated in both directions, honestly:
U-Shares without an intelligence endowment leaves the periphery renting the instrument; an intelligence
endowment without a property leg leaves compounding ownership where it already concentrates.
Directional; a comparison, not an endorsement.
