# Evidence — F3: The rule is only as sound as its baseline

**Claim (register):** The "above-baseline receipts only" rule is only as sound as the baseline it draws
against.
**Status: `open`.**
**Warrant (proposed):** the rule's integrity reduces to the baseline's integrity — a draw defined against
a counterfactual is only as honest as the counterfactual's definition and governance.

## The problem, stated precisely
"Receipts above a pre-AI baseline" requires a counterfactual no one observes. Three failure modes:
1. **Manipulation.** Whoever sets the baseline can set it low (fire the trigger on noise) or high (never
   fire). An honest rule needs a baseline authority and method fixed *in advance* and hard to revise —
   the same lawful-redefinition vulnerability documented for statutory formulas (GV1: the PFD lesson).
2. **Identification (the WS-4 sharpening).** The AI-*attributable* component of receipts growth may be
   an order of magnitude smaller than CBO's own forecast error — in which case an honest trigger rarely
   fires and a permissive one fires on noise. No attribution methodology yet exists that separates
   AI-driven receipts from ordinary forecast variance at the horizon the program needs.
3. **Drift.** Even an honestly-set baseline ages; re-basing rules (when, by whom, reviewable how) are
   themselves governance surface.

## What exists vs what's missing
The models (F1/F2) *assume* a sound baseline and demonstrate behavior conditional on it. What does not
exist: a specified, reproducible baseline methodology; an attribution method clearing the forecast-noise
floor; a governance design for the baseline authority. The register's disconfirming test is written so
that a proof of *impossibility* retires the funding rule honestly — while a workable design (OQ-FIS-002's
answer-shape) would move F2 forward. Either answer is valuable; that is why this claim ships `open`.

## Disconfirming test (register)
A demonstration that no non-manipulable counterfactual baseline can be defined and governed.
