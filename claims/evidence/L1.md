# Evidence — L1: Equity-not-debt classification (honest-open; a counsel gate)

**Claim (register):** A fixed, inflation-indexed, enforceable 5% floor can be structured as a genuine
ownership interest (equity), not debt.
**Status: `open`** — and it will remain open at launch: **no counsel opinion exists, and none will by
July.** This file states the question precisely and what rides on it; it does not argue an answer.

**Warrant (proposed):** REIT §857 and preferred-equity practice show deductible fixed-coupon ownership
forms are constructible in US law, but only a tax/securities counsel opinion can establish the
classification — the claim stays honestly open until one exists.

## The question, precisely
The U-Share carries a **mandatory, inflation-indexed 5% coupon on issue price**, enforceable by the
holder, backstopped by an insurer with issuer recourse. Debt-vs-equity classification is a multi-factor
doctrine (fixed obligation, enforcement rights, seniority, participation, intent) applied differently
across **tax** (deductibility depends on the instrument being what it claims), **securities**,
**bankruptcy** (priority), and **property/Takings** contexts. An instrument this floor-like invites
debt-recharacterization; classification must survive in *each* body of law.

## The analogs (why the claim is plausible enough to register)
- **REIT §857:** deductible dividends on equity — the deduction-on-equity precedent U-Shares extends.
- **Preferred equity:** fixed cumulative coupons, enforceable in constrained ways, still equity — showing
  fixed-coupon ownership exists; but preferred dividends are *not* deductible and not indexed, so the
  analog is partial in exactly the dimensions that matter.
- A **congressionally-defined instrument** (the actual proposal) can specify its own tax treatment —
  which answers the *tax* question by statute but not the securities/bankruptcy/Takings ones, and invites
  the lawful-redefinition durability concern (GV1) about anything statute gives, statute can rework.

## What rides on the answer
If the floor forces **debt** treatment: the REIT-style deduction framing collapses into ordinary interest
(mechanically similar cost, very different framing and law); holders become creditors (bankruptcy
priority changes — arguably *better* for holders, worse for the ownership thesis); the F4 permanence
argument (equity-not-debt) needs restating; and the instrument's "citizen ownership" identity — the
program's point — is impaired. The claim's stakes are structural, which is why the register carries it
rather than footnoting it.

## Disconfirming test (register)
A tax/securities counsel opinion that an enforceable fixed floor forces debt (not equity) treatment.
