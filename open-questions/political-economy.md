# Open Questions — Political-Economy / Capture-Durability Skeptic (OQ-PE)

**Persona.** Public funds are sunk by capture, not markets. Can this be built un-raidable — and if the
answer is "it's a narrow insurer, so there's little to raid," does that hold under real political
pressure, over decades, without incentive erosion? And why hasn't something like this already happened?
**How to engage.** Claim via Issue (`question-claim.yml`) citing the OQ-ID. Claim links → `CLAIMS.md`.
*(Maintainer-written, contributor-read.)*

---

### OQ-PE-001 — Capture-resistant governance design
- **Question.** What governance design keeps a public fund un-raidable across administrations, benchmarked
  against the capture base-rate (1MDB, Venezuela FONDEN, Libya LIA)?
- **What an answer would look like.** A concrete governance specification (independence, transparency,
  on-budget accountability, statutory/constitutional anti-raid protection) with a stated mechanism for why
  it resists a deficit-running sovereign — and, ideally, a scored comparison against failed and durable
  funds.
- **Links to claim.** F6 (safety reserve) · GV1 (governance/durability).
- **Route.** Extension / empirical (a design proposal is an extension; contradicting base-rate evidence falsifies).

### OQ-PE-002 — Incentive erosion & "why hasn't this happened?"
- **Question.** Does the incentive structure resist hollowing or gaming over decades — and why has
  comparable broad-ownership-via-issuance not already emerged?
- **What an answer would look like.** A political-economy analysis of the adoption barriers and of the
  durability of the incentives — the points at which **issuer**, **holder**, or **fiscal** incentives
  erode over time — and an account of the historical/institutional barriers that have blocked similar
  mechanisms.
- **Links to claim.** F1 (funding path) · F6 (safety reserve) · GV1 (governance/durability —
  political-feasibility facet).
- **Route.** Extension (commentary / analysis).
